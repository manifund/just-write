# API printer mails, rush printer proofs

No single printer found meets every requirement, so the best plan uses two: **RPI Print's Self-Service API** (RPI is the company behind Blurb and MagCloud) to print and mail one copy per member, and a rush short-run printer, **Printivity**, for the proof copy. RPI is self-serve with a sandbox, takes one API call per recipient with URLs to a cover PDF and an interior PDF, and by the researchers' arithmetic delivers a full-color, perfect-bound 8.5 x 11 in copy on 100 lb coated paper for **about $21 at 100 pages and $26 at 150 pages**, roughly **6-10 calendar days** after the file is sent. None of the print-on-demand services can put a proof in hand in 1-3 days (their best case is about 4 business days), which is why the proof has to come from a rush printer; Printivity displayed next-day delivery for a single perfect-bound copy priced at $52.60 before rush and shipping charges. That proof checks layout, type and image resolution but not RPI's color or binding, so the template itself should be proofed once on RPI and then held fixed. RPI ships only to the US and Canada, so the few overseas copies and any stock for sale come from a small extra batch sent to the organizer, and public sales can run through the site's existing Stripe checkout with one RPI order per purchase. The printer expects two files per issue: an interior PDF of single pages at trim size plus 0.125 in bleed, with fonts embedded and images at 300 ppi, and a separate one-piece cover PDF (back, spine, front); an sRGB PDF from headless Chromium is acceptable to the print-on-demand services. The main unknowns (RPI's actual print quality and November turnaround, its default cap of 50 orders a day, Printivity's rush fee) need a paid test order this week, not more desk research.

> **How figures are labelled.** "Confirmed" means a researcher read the figure on the provider's own page, calculator or catalog on 2026-11-10. "Computed" or "estimate" means arithmetic on confirmed inputs, such as a print price plus a published shipping rate. "Inference" is a researcher's judgment or mine. "Search snippet only" means the provider's page blocked automated reading (Mixam's, Peecho's and Blurb's support pages, Midjourney's docs and terms, and a few others), so the text came from a search engine's quote of that page and should be re-checked before ordering. Prices are USD before sales tax. Quotes that needed a ZIP used San Francisco (94103) or Chicago (60601); the organizer's actual location was not confirmed.

## Perfect binding on 80-100 lb paper is the realistic format

At 80-150 pages, every printer checked means perfect binding: a glued square spine with a wraparound cover. Saddle stitch stops at **48 pages at Smartpress, 96 at Mixam and 100 at MagCloud** ([Smartpress](https://smartpress.com/support/product-faq/booklet-printing-faq), [Mixam](https://mixam.com/magazines), [MagCloud](https://www.magcloud.com/help/faq/item/1004)). Thread-sewn binding, which opens flatter, is "rare for short-run printing", and one vendor lists a 500-book minimum (search snippet only) ([OnPress](https://www.onpressbookprinting.com/resources/book-binding-options)). Within perfect binding the glue matters. Hemlock, the printer credited on Asterisk, says standard EVA glue "can become brittle over time" and "may not bond as effectively to coated papers or heavier stocks", while PUR is more flexible; a binder manufacturer adds that digital toner in the spine area weakens ordinary hot-melt glue ([Hemlock](https://www.hemlock.com/blog/2026/03/12/perfect-binding-production-decisions-that-shape-a-book/), [Morgana](https://www.mybinding.com/media/manuals/Morgana-DigiBook-200-Spec-Sheet.pdf)). Mixam and Smartpress state that they use PUR ([Mixam](https://mixam.com/magazines), [Smartpress](https://smartpress.com/support/product-faq/booklet-printing-faq)). RPI's catalog says only "Glue Bound (Standard)", so its glue type is unknown ([RPI catalog](https://products.api.rpiprint.com/catalog/v1/products.md)).

Paper is the next lever. One US printer's guide calls 80 lb gloss text "the industry standard for consumer magazines in 2026" and 100 lb a premium feel for art magazines; it describes matte or silk as easier to read under direct light, uncoated as warmer and suited to literary titles, and coated stock as necessary for saturated images. The same guide recommends a 100 lb cover at least one grade heavier than the interior, with lamination as the premium finish ([Publishing Xpress](https://www.publishingxpress.com/blog/how-to-choose-paper-stock-for-magazine-printing/)). What can actually be ordered depends on the type of printer. Print-on-demand catalogs are short: RPI offers 70 lb uncoated and three 100 lb "Premium" papers (gloss, dull, eggshell) with a 10 pt gloss or matte cover; Lulu offers 60# uncoated or 80# coated in "Standard" or "Premium" color; MagCloud uses 80# text with a UV-coated cover ([RPI](https://www.rpiprint.com/products-services/print-apis/self-service), [Lulu calculator](https://developers.lulu.com/price-calculator), [MagCloud](https://www.magcloud.com/help/faq/item/1002)). Short-run printers offer more: Mixam and Printivity both list uncoated and coated text papers up to 100 lb, and cover finishes that include matte and soft-touch lamination ([Mixam](https://mixam.com/magazines), [Printivity](https://www.printivity.com/books/perfect-bound-books)).

Press type and tolerances set the ceiling on quality. A book printer that runs both technologies says HP Indigo gives "higher print quality than inkjet", with brighter color and more contrast, and that the two are close for black text ([Friesens](https://www.friesens.com/blog/digital-inkjet-vs-toner-whats-the-difference/)). MagCloud says it prints on HP Indigo; Mixam's calculator named an Indigo press for short jobs but **a "Canon iX" press, which the researchers identify as inkjet, for the 100-152 page jobs quoted here**; RPI's press for API orders is not stated ([MagCloud](https://www.magcloud.com/help/faq/item/1124), [Mixam](https://mixam.com/magazines)). Print-on-demand also drifts: stated trim tolerance is 1/8 in at Lulu and 1/16 in at IngramSpark, and one publisher received ten copies in three different cover colors ([AuthorImprints](https://www.authorimprints.com/pod-print-quality-problem-examples/)). The practical design rules follow from that: wide margins, no borders near the trim, no art across the gutter, and a spine in the same color as the covers. The researchers' inferred baseline for this job is PUR perfect binding, 80-100 lb matte or silk coated text, and a matte-laminated cover; this is assembled from the sources above, not one source's recommendation.

### Trim size changes postage only when the organizer mails the copies

Print-on-demand shipping is priced per order, not per ounce. Lulu's Mail rate stayed at $5.69 for both a 100-page and a 150-page Letter copy, and RPI charges $6.99 for the first item in an order on Standard shipping ([Lulu calculator](https://developers.lulu.com/price-calculator), [RPI rates](https://open.api.rpiprint.com/shipping/rates)). On those routes trim size affects the print price and the paper list instead. At RPI, **6 x 9 softcover is offered only on 70 lb uncoated paper; coated paper means 8 x 10 or 8.5 x 11** ([RPI catalog](https://products.api.rpiprint.com/catalog/v1/products.md)). At Lulu, premium color costs $15.88 (100 pp) and $22.83 (150 pp) at 6 x 9 against $23.64 and $34.38 at Letter ([Lulu calculator](https://developers.lulu.com/price-calculator)).

When the organizer mails copies by hand, weight tiers decide the cost. The weights below come from Mixam's calculator for 80 lb text with a 100 lb cover; heavier paper weighs more, and the mailed weights add an assumed 1 oz for the envelope.

The **13 oz ceiling on First-Class large envelopes** is the constraint. A flexible 6 x 9 copy under it mails for $4-5 in 1-5 days without tracking; anything heavier or rigid pays parcel prices ([USPS Notice 123](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/Notice123.htm), [USPS services](https://www.usps.com/ship/mail-shipping-services.htm)). The researchers estimate that 6 x 9 saves $3-4 per copy against Ground Advantage, or $300-400 per issue. Media Mail ($4.39 at 1 lb, 2-8 days) is uncertain for a dated weekly and should be treated as a fallback: its rules cover books "consisting wholly of reading matter" that are not entered as Periodicals, and it is subject to inspection ([USPS DMM 173](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/173.htm)). Marketing Mail and Periodicals need permits and fees ($390 annual fee; $1,170 Periodicals application) that do not suit 100 copies a week ([USPS Notice 123](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/Notice123.htm)). Whether label services such as Pirate Ship can sell large-envelope postage was not confirmed; stamps or a meter may be needed.

Three patterns stand out. First, price and speed pull apart at the cheap end: Mixam's bulk price is **$5.88 to $9.74 per copy** depending on size and page count, but a Tuesday order ships Friday and reaches San Francisco by ground the following Friday, so hand-mailed copies land about two weeks after the file ([Mixam](https://mixam.com/magazines)). Second, the services that mail each copy directly and fit the budget differ mostly in time: RPI's documented schedule is 4 business days of production plus 2-5 days in transit, Lulu's own estimate is 11-14 business days by Mail, and MagCloud's calculator showed 13 calendar days ([RPI shipping docs](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/shipping), [Lulu calculator](https://developers.lulu.com/price-calculator), [MagCloud calculator](https://www.magcloud.com/formats/pricingcalculator)). Third, only two kinds of route are near one week inside the budget: RPI's API, and a 1-2 day printer shipping the run to the organizer for hand-mailing. No route was confirmed end to end at seven days, and Lulu warns that production slows late in the year, so November orders may run behind these figures ([Lulu guide](https://assets.lulu.com/media/guides/en/lulu-getting-started-guide.pdf)).

On API fit, Lulu and RPI are the two that can be used today. Lulu issues keys on sign-up and has a separate sandbox; RPI accounts start in sandbox mode with no approval step mentioned and need a card on file for production ([Lulu API docs](https://api.lulu.com/docs/), [RPI getting started](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/getting-started)). Both take one authenticated POST per recipient with PDF URLs, a shipping address and a shipping level, and both send status and tracking by webhook. Mixam has a public API and a print-on-demand product, but API access requires an approved account, and neither its per-address mailing cost nor its print-on-demand pricing was visible without logging in ([Mixam API docs](https://mixam.com/documentation/api/public)). MagCloud has no API that the researchers could find, but its Ship to Group feature mails one publication to a saved address group in a single order ([MagCloud](https://www.magcloud.com/help/faq/item/1032)). No public API was found for Printivity, DocuCopies, 48 Hour Books or PrintingCenterUSA, though this was not checked exhaustively.

Evidence on print quality is thin and mostly anecdotal. The best side-by-side test found is from 2016: it rated Lulu's standard color "one of the worst" and its premium color "one of the best", and Blurb's premium products among the best ([Visualizing Architecture](https://visualizingarchitecture.com/battle-of-p-o-d-printers-2016/)). One zine maker wrote in April 2026 that moving from MagCloud to Blurb gave heavier, better paper ([boats16](https://boats16.blogspot.com/2026/04/zine-vs-zine.html)), while Blurb's Trustpilot page has 2026 complaints of flat, low-contrast images ([Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/blurb.co.uk)). These reviews concern Blurb's consumer products, not the RPI API's SKUs, so they are weak evidence for the recommended route. Community views of Mixam (reliable for short runs, prices rising, US quality "at best acceptable" per one poster) came from search-result summaries only.

Several providers were ruled out. Newspaper Club prints newsprint up to 64 pages, and PrintNinja is offset with a 250-copy minimum and 2-3 weeks of production ([Newspaper Club](https://www.newspaperclub.com/), [PrintNinja](https://printninja.com/turnaround-times/)). Gelato and Prodigi sell photo books, not magazines, and Printful and Printify appear to have no magazine product, though that rests on a secondary listing and their catalogs were not checked ([Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/products/travel-photo-books), [Prodigi](http://www.prodigi.com/faq/print-api/), [OJ Digital Solutions](https://ojdigitalsolutions.com/print-on-demand-journals/)). Bookvault charges a per-title setup fee of $24.95 on its free plan, which a weekly would presumably pay for every issue ([Bookvault](https://bookvault.app/cost-to-print-your-book/)). Edition One Books' website had expired on 2026-11-10 ([Edition One](https://www.editiononebooks.com/)).

## Recommended: RPI's API for the run, Printivity for the proof

### Primary route: one RPI order per member

RPI is the recommendation because it is the only self-serve API found whose coated-paper, Letter-size product stays under $30 delivered at 150 pages, and its documented schedule is the closest of the direct-mail services to one week. The order call is `POST https://open.api.rpiprint.com/orders/create` with a shipping class, a webhook URL, one destination, and an item carrying a SKU, a `coverUrl` and a `gutsUrl`; a standard manufacturing SKU can be ordered without setting up a product first, and packages are unbranded ([RPI order request](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/first-request), [RPI getting started](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/getting-started), [Blurb Print API](https://www.blurb.com/print-api-software)). Pricing is a base plus a per-page rate: **$7.00 for the first 20 pages plus $0.09 per page** at 8.5 x 11 on 100 lb gloss or dull, and $7.20 plus $0.09 at 8 x 10 ([RPI catalog](https://products.api.rpiprint.com/catalog/v1/products.md)). That makes 100 copies cost about $2,120 per issue at 100 pages and $2,570 at 150 pages (computed). The suggested spec is 8 x 10 or 8.5 x 11 on 100 lb Premium Dull with a matte cover; the choice of dull over gloss is an inference from the paper guidance above, to be settled by test copies. The 100 lb eggshell paper costs $9.20 plus $0.17 per page, which by my arithmetic is about $29.79 delivered at 100 pages and $38.29 at 150, so it fits the budget only for short issues.

Four operating details need attention before the first run. RPI accepts **50 orders per rolling 24 hours by default**; further orders are queued, so 100 members means either a day's delay for half of them or a limit increase from support, which should be requested now ([RPI orders docs](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/orders.md)). Cards are charged once a day at 02:00 UTC unless "Pay Now" is used in the dashboard, and production days count from when the printer receives the order, so unpaid orders lose a day. Order creation is not idempotent and a duplicate `customerOrderId` returns HTTP 400, so a fixed id per issue and member makes retries safe (inference). And self-service accounts ship only to the US and Canada, with no military addresses ([RPI shipping docs](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/shipping)). Lulu's API requires a phone number on every shipping address and RPI's destination object includes one, so the address form should collect it ([Lulu API docs](https://api.lulu.com/docs/)).

The risk in this route is that nobody has seen an RPI copy of this magazine. The glue, the press and the real November turnaround are unknown, and RPI offers no free samples ([RPI support FAQ](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/support-main.md)). If the first issue has to go out before a test copy arrives, that issue is the test, and its layout should be conservative.

### Fast proofs: a single rush copy from Printivity

No print-on-demand service meets the 1-3 day requirement. The best cases found were about 4 business days: RPI Rush (3 business days of production plus 1 day of transit, $35.99 shipping), MagCloud with FedEx overnight, and Mixam with next-day air, where a copy ordered Tuesday arrives the following Monday ([RPI rates](https://open.api.rpiprint.com/shipping/rates), [MagCloud calculator](https://www.magcloud.com/formats/pricingcalculator), [Mixam](https://mixam.com/magazines)). Lulu's Express estimate is 5-8 business days.

Rush short-run printers can meet it. On Tuesday 2026-11-10 Printivity priced **one 152-page 6 x 9 perfect-bound copy at $52.60** before shipping and rush, and showed "Want it Wednesday, November 11th?"; it also sells a phone-quoted "Rush Critical" service for same-day and overnight work ([Printivity](https://www.printivity.com/books/perfect-bound-books), [Printivity Rush Critical](https://www.printivity.com/rush-critical)). The rush surcharge, overnight shipping cost and production location were not captured. DocuCopies showed the same next-day option and has a California plant, but a single copy cost about $270 ([DocuCopies](https://www.docucopies.com/order/PERFECT-BOUND-BOOKS/), [DocuCopies rush](https://www.docucopies.com/rush-printing-services/)). 48 Hour Books has a 10-book minimum and charges $395 for one-day production ([48 Hour Books](https://www.48hrbooks.com/book-printing/pricing-calculator)). A local shop might be faster still, but the Bay Area leads (ABC Imaging, AlphaGraphics, Copy Edge, 1984 Printing) came from search summaries and none was confirmed to do same-day perfect binding ([ABC Imaging](https://abcimaging.com/abc-imaging-san-francisco-ca), [MetaFilter](https://ask.metafilter.com/341698/Quick-turnaround-perfect-binding-in-Bay-Area-like-Espresso-Book-Machine)); nothing current was found for FedEx Office or Staples.

A Printivity proof is a different object from an RPI copy. It verifies margins, type size, page order, image sharpness and typos, not RPI's color, paper or binding. The workable division is to proof the template once on RPI itself, by Rush order, and hold fonts, colors and image treatment fixed afterwards; then use the next-day proof for each issue's content while the template is new. A per-issue proof also costs a day: with it, members receive copies roughly 7-11 days after the file is final (estimate). Whether to keep it after the first few issues is a choice between the two timing goals.

### Selling copies and reaching international members

For sales, the simplest path is a checkout on the existing site that places one RPI order per paid purchase; the site already takes payments through Stripe, and RPI has no storefront of its own (inference from [Blurb Print API](https://www.blurb.com/print-api-software)). The delivered cost of $21-26 is the floor for a US sale price. If a hosted store is preferred later, Lulu Direct connects Shopify, WooCommerce or Wix to Lulu's printers, and Mixam gives every print-on-demand product a shareable "PrintLink" ([Lulu](https://www.lulu.com/sell/sell-on-your-site), [Mixam](https://mixam.com/print-on-demand)). Lulu Direct's Shopify app has ratings around 2.7-3.2 stars, with complaints about slow production and untracked international shipping ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/lulu-direct)).

Selling also raises rights questions that distribution to members alone does not. Each writer should give written permission for a non-exclusive print reprint (inference, not legal advice). For Midjourney art, a secondary legal guide says paid plans allow commercial use and that a company with more than $1,000,000 in annual revenue must be on a Pro or Mega plan to own its assets; Midjourney's own terms page blocked reading, so this needs checking against the live terms and against the revenue of the entity that publishes the magazine ([Terms.law](https://terms.law/ai-output-rights/midjourney/)). Sales tax on sold copies was not researched.

For members outside the US, the recommendation is to add one multi-copy RPI order to the organizer each week and post the overseas copies by hand. RPI's per-item shipping falls to $2.50 for items 2-5 and $2.00 for items 6-10 within one order, so ten extra copies add about $2.70 each in shipping (computed from [RPI rates](https://open.api.rpiprint.com/shipping/rates)). The same batch supplies spares and copies for in-person sales. Canada can go through RPI directly at $17.99 for the first item, with duties paid by the recipient ([RPI shipping docs](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/shipping)). USPS First-Class Mail International large envelopes cost **$12.95-$18.55 for 8-16 oz** to the UK, Germany, France, Australia and Japan, and $6.65-$8.55 to Canada ([USPS Notice 123](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/Notice123.htm)). A Letter-size copy on 100 lb paper will probably weigh more than the 15.994 oz at which that table stops (inference from Mixam's weights for lighter paper). Rigid or heavier pieces pay package prices, which start at $26.00 for 9-16 oz in the cheapest country group and were not priced above a pound, so the actual weight and price need checking with a real copy. The alternative is a Lulu order per overseas member, since Lulu prints through a global network, but international rates and transit times were not priced for any provider, and Lulu needs its own file set and gives those members a different object.

### Fallbacks if RPI's test copies disappoint

If RPI's quality or timing falls short, the next choice is the hand-mail route: Printivity (or 48 Hour Books) prints 100 copies at 6 x 9 in 1-2 days, ships them to the organizer, and the organizer mails them as First-Class large envelopes. That costs about $16-24 per copy and takes about 5-9 days by estimate, with a proof from the same printer as the run, but it has no API and means packing 100 envelopes every week. DocuCopies is the hands-off version: it is the only printer found that will print perfect-bound books and mail each one in a padded envelope to an uploaded list, at about $3.27 per book plus postage, typically mailing in 3-5 business days ([DocuCopies](https://www.docucopies.com/order/PERFECT-BOUND-BOOKS/)). MagCloud's Ship to Group is the no-code print-on-demand fallback, and Mixam is the low-cost option if issues can run two weeks behind.

## Expect to send two PDFs: a single-page interior with bleed and a one-piece cover

Every provider checked wants the same basic package: a PDF of single pages (not spreads) at trim size plus **0.125 in bleed**, fonts embedded, images at 300 ppi, and usually no crop marks, with the cover as a separate one-piece spread of back cover, spine and front cover ([Lulu](https://help.api.lulu.com/en/support/solutions/articles/64000254609-pdf-creation-settings), [PrintingCenterUSA](https://www.printingcenterusa.com/help/file-preparation)). On the RPI route the remaining "file" is the order payload per member: name, address, phone, email, SKU, quantity and shipping class, with the two PDFs hosted at URLs the printer can fetch. A print-and-mail printer such as DocuCopies takes a CSV instead, with columns for name, company, two address lines, city, two-letter state and ZIP ([DocuCopies](https://www.docucopies.com/mailing-services/)).

The details differ by printer in ways that affect layout code. The table is the researchers' summary; the Peecho, Mixam and Blurb rows rest on search snippets.

| Smartpress | plain PDF | not confirmed | single pages | separate file | crop marks wanted |

Sources: [Lulu](https://help.api.lulu.com/en/support/solutions/articles/64000254609-pdf-creation-settings), [Blurb](https://www.blurb.com/pdf-to-book), [Blurb Affinity guide](https://support.blurb.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044955872-Affinity-Publisher-PDF-files), [Peecho](https://support.peecho.com/hc/en-us/articles/19746186078876-Magazines-File-set-up-guideline), [Mixam](https://mixam.com/support/filesetup), [PrintingCenterUSA](https://www.printingcenterusa.com/help/file-preparation), [Smartpress](https://smartpress.com/support/file-preparation/how-to-setup-your-file).

The researchers did not read RPI's own file specification for API orders, which is a gap for the recommended route. Blurb's consumer rules are the nearest evidence: an even page count, 100% black ink for text, no trim marks, a separate cover made after the page count is final, and dimensions taken from Blurb's calculator ([Blurb](https://www.blurb.com/pdf-to-book)). RPI's API has an unauthenticated template generator that returns cover and page templates for a given SKU and page count, plus preflight checks, so the exact dimensions and spine width can be read from the service before any layout is final ([RPI products docs](https://docs.api.rpiprint.com/documentation/products)). Because bleed rules differ (all four sides at Lulu, none on the inside edge at Blurb, none at all at Peecho), page size and bleed should be export settings per printer, not constants.

Lulu's guide is the most complete published rule set and a reasonable default for margins. It asks for a 0.5 in safety margin, an inside margin of 0.625 in at 61-150 pages, total ink coverage under 270%, and solid black at 100% K; its paperback spine is (pages / 444) + 0.06 in, about 0.29 in at 100 pages and 0.40 in at 150, with no spine text at 80 pages or fewer ([Lulu Book Creation Guide](https://assets.lulu.com/media/guides/en/lulu-book-creation-guide.pdf)). Mixam and Printivity ask for a 0.5 in gutter margin (search snippets). Spine width depends on the paper and differs by printer, so the cover has to be regenerated once the page count is known. An even page count satisfies most printers for perfect binding; a multiple of 4 keeps Mixam available.

Color is the main place where a web pipeline and print expectations diverge. Lulu says its printers "prefer files created using sRGB" and advises against converting; Mixam and PrintingCenterUSA prefer CMYK but convert RGB automatically, with Mixam warning that "RGB files may produce unexpected results" ([Lulu](https://help.api.lulu.com/en/support/solutions/articles/64000254609-pdf-creation-settings), [Mixam](https://mixam.com/support/filesetup)). A browser writes black text as RGB 0,0,0, so whether body text prints in black ink only depends on the printer's conversion; this is something to inspect on the first proof.

Art needs more pixels than Midjourney delivers by default. A full-bleed page at 300 ppi is 1875 x 2775 px at 6 x 9, 2475 x 3075 px at 8 x 10 and 2625 x 3375 px at 8.5 x 11 (arithmetic). Midjourney's upscalers take a default image to 2048 x 2048 px (search snippet of its docs), which the researchers work out to roughly 210-270 ppi on a full page: above the 150-180 ppi floors that Lulu and PrintingCenterUSA quote, below 300 ([Midjourney docs](https://docs.midjourney.com/docs/upscalers), [Lulu Book Creation Guide](https://assets.lulu.com/media/guides/en/lulu-book-creation-guide.pdf)). One more 1.5-2x pass in a separate upscaler closes the gap for full-page and cover art. Images supplied by authors or taken from their essays are the larger risk, since web images are often 1000-1500 px wide (inference).

## Headless Chromium fits the web preview; CMYK engines are optional

No browser-based tool produces CMYK or PDF/X on its own, and for the recommended printers that does not matter, because the print-on-demand services accept sRGB. That makes headless Chromium the natural renderer: the preview in the browser and the exported PDF then come from the same layout engine. Chrome 131 added page margin boxes and page counters, so page numbers work natively; running headers, page-number cross-references and footnotes are still unsupported, and Chrome offers no control over the PDF's color space ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/print-margins), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39670470)). Chromium's support for bleed and crop-mark descriptors was not confirmed, so the safe approach is to set the page size to trim plus bleed directly, which is also what printers that reject crop marks want.

This magazine's structure reduces the usual difficulties. Write-ups of books typeset from HTML report trouble with long flowing text: pagination bugs, weak justification and footnotes ([Hypermedia Systems](https://deniz.aksimsek.tr/2024/new-hypermedia-systems), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35242299)). A magazine of one page or excerpt per writer can instead be built as fixed-size page components in React, each exactly one printed page, with notes kept inside the piece. That is my inference, not a tested result. If essays later flow across many pages with footnotes and running headers, Vivliostyle is the better-maintained addition: its CLI reached version 10 in December 2025, has a preview server, bleed and crop-mark options, and a post-processing step that swaps RGB values for CMYK with documented limits ([Vivliostyle](https://vivliostyle.org/), [Vivliostyle docs](https://docs.vivliostyle.org/en/cli/special-output-settings/)). Paged.js gives a facing-pages preview inside the page most directly, but its last stable release is from July 2023, a breaking 2.0 is planned, and one book team called it "slow, buggy, and machine-dependent" ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/pagedjs), [Paged.js](https://pagedjs.org/posts/en/a-new-chapter-in-paged.js-life/)).

The CMYK-capable engines are the escalation path, not the starting point. Prince converts colors to an output profile, writes PDF/X-1a, X-3 or X-4, and by default maps RGB greys to black ink only; it costs $495 for a desktop licence or $3,800 per server, and its free non-commercial licence puts a logo on the first page and excludes documents that generate income ([Prince](https://www.princexml.com/doc/graphics/), [Prince pricing](https://www.princexml.com/purchase/), [Prince licence FAQ](https://www.princexml.com/purchase/license_faq/)). DocRaptor hosts Prince behind an HTTP API for a per-document price that the researchers could not retrieve ([DocRaptor](https://docraptor.com/)). WeasyPrint added PDF/X and CMYK recently, and in April 2026 a manufacturer's preflight rejected one of its PDF/X-4 files over an RGB transparency group ([WeasyPrint issue](https://github.com/Kozea/WeasyPrint/issues/2723)). With any of these the browser preview is only approximate, because line and page breaks come from a different engine. They become worth it if a proof shows color or black-text problems, or if the run moves to a printer that prefers CMYK, such as Mixam or Printivity. Typst has no PDF/X output and is not an HTML renderer, and react-pdf would mean a second layout codebase.

Rendering should run in a sandbox or CI job, not a request-scoped Vercel function. Vercel functions have a 250 MB bundle limit and a 4.5 MB response cap, Vercel's own guide requires a slimmed Chromium build, and that build ships a single font ([Vercel limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations), [Vercel guide](https://vercel.com/guides/deploying-puppeteer-with-nextjs-on-vercel), [Sparticuz/chromium](https://github.com/Sparticuz/chromium)). A 150-page color PDF will be far larger than 4.5 MB (inference), so the job should write it to file storage and pass the URL to the printer. The site already starts Vercel Sandboxes for its @Claude runs, so the pattern exists in the codebase.

Automated checks cover part of what a proof does. Open tools verify that fonts are embedded (`pdffonts`), that placed images have enough resolution (`pdfimages -list`), and that every page is the expected size ([pdffonts](https://dyn.manpages.debian.org/trixie/poppler-utils/pdffonts.1)). No open-source tool validates PDF/X, and Ghostscript's RGB-to-CMYK conversion has known problems with transparency ([Ghostscript](https://ghostscript.readthedocs.io/en/latest/VectorDevices.html)). The printers' own checks are the practical preflight: Lulu exposes `/validate-interior` and `/validate-cover` endpoints, and RPI runs preflight on submitted files ([Lulu OpenAPI spec](https://api.lulu.com/api-docs/openapi-specs/openapi_public.yml)).

## The inspirations: one on-demand spec, one offset quarterly, one subsidized monthly

**Offprint** is the closest in production method. Its site states an 8.5 x 11 in trim, at least 96 pages (padded with ruled notes pages), up to 320, coated stock, a gloss cover and full color; its Creator Edition page calls the product perfect-bound ([Offprint](https://getoffprint.com/), [Offprint Creator Edition](https://getoffprint.com/lenny)). It charges $39 a month with shipping included and delivers 10 business days after the reader approves the issue. The printer is not named anywhere the researchers could read: the terms refer only to "our printer", and the founder's posts on X could not be opened ([Offprint terms](https://getoffprint.com/terms)). Because every copy is unique, it must be one-off digital printing, and the spec matches the standard magazine product of mainstream print-on-demand vendors; both points are inferences. The site says "the final formatting is checked before every magazine prints", so even this automated pipeline keeps a manual review.

**Asterisk** gets its quality from design and an offset printer. An AIGA competition entry credits MGMT. design (Sarah Gephart) for design, Hemlock as printer, the typefaces Atlas Grotesk and Noe, the paper Neenah Classic Crest, and 6-8 commissioned illustrators per issue, with a production budget band of $30,001-$100,000 ([AIGA 365](https://aiga-365-design-competition.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/343/details/68138)). That entry is from the 2023 competition, so paper and printer may have changed. Single issues sell for $19 and a US subscription is $80 a year ([Asterisk store](https://store.asteriskmag.com/)). Trim size, page count, binding and print run are not published. Classic Crest is an uncoated line, so the interior is almost certainly uncoated (inference). Each issue also gets a new asterisk mark that is reused as an end mark throughout ([MGMT](https://www.mgmtdesign.com/work/asterisk-magazine)).

**Midjourney Magazine** is 116 pages at 8.5 x 11 in for $4 a month, shipped once a month, with delivery taking from one to more than six weeks ([Midjourney Magazine](https://mag.midjourney.com/midjourneymag), [support](https://mag.midjourney.com/support)). That price almost certainly does not cover cost (inference), so it is no benchmark. Paper, binding and printer are not stated officially. A Lithuanian printer, KOPA, has a portfolio entry titled "Midjourney"; a search summary of that page listed 130 gsm gloss-coated text, a 250 gsm cover and sewn softcover binding, but the specs did not appear when the page was fetched, so the link is circumstantial ([KOPA](https://druckerei-kopa.de/midjourney)). If accurate, that is an offset-run spec that would be costly at 100 copies.

What carries over is mostly design, not print specification. Asterisk, and Works in Progress on satin-coated stock from Park|Graphius, both rely on a small fixed type system, generous margins and a recurring device per issue, all of which a web template can reproduce ([Park|Graphius](https://parkgraphius.co.uk/case-studies/works-in-progress-business-magazine-printing/)). The two paper approaches are premium uncoated (Asterisk) and satin or gloss coated (Works in Progress, Offprint, Midjourney); for a full-page artwork per piece, matte or silk coated is the usual compromise (inference). Wired's review of Midjourney's magazine was headlined "Soulless" (search snippet only), which suggests letting each writer supply or approve the art for their piece.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which printer" to "which two". Speed for one copy and automation for a hundred are sold by different kinds of company: rush shops with web forms and next-day production, and API services with production queues of several days. The budget is not what limits the plan. RPI's delivered price leaves $4-9 per copy under the $30 ceiling, and a Printivity proof at its $52.60 base price adds about $0.53 per copy across 100 copies before rush and shipping charges. Time is the limit: the documented schedule puts each issue in mailboxes about when the next one goes to print, and a hard proof per issue adds a day on top.

Two design decisions follow. Page count is the main cost control: at RPI's $0.09 per page, 50 pages change an issue's bill by about $450. And the export code should treat the printer as a setting (trim, bleed convention, spine width, color handling), so that moving the run from RPI to a hand-mailed 6 x 9 printing, or adding Lulu for overseas members, is a configuration change. The remaining uncertainty is physical. It is resolved by holding RPI copies and a Printivity copy of the same file. By my arithmetic that is about $50 per RPI Rush test order (print price plus $35.99 shipping) and $52.60 plus rush charges at Printivity, and it takes about a week.
